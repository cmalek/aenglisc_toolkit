# The Institutes of Polity (2)
Source: Rabin (ed.) (2020). Old English Legal Writings: Wulfstan. Harvard University Press, pp. 60-97.
Translator: Christopher Malek
Notes: Wulfstan composed and revised the materials now called the Institutes of Polity during the early eleventh century, when he served as Archbishop of York and as an adviser and lawmaker under Æthelred II and Cnut. Responding to Scandinavian invasions, dynastic upheaval, and perceived moral decline, he imagined a “holy society” in which kings, bishops, clergy, nobles, workers, and warriors fulfilled mutually supporting duties under God.
The B manuscript itself (this manuscript) was produced at Worcester around 1060–1072, roughly forty years after Wulfstan’s death, during the episcopate of Wulfstan of Worcester. Its version of the Institutes combines material associated with earlier and later stages of the work and has often been treated as the fullest surviving representation of Archbishop Wulfstan’s political vision. It begins with God as the heavenly king and proceeds through the various ranks of Christian society, presenting earthly government as part of a divinely ordered hierarchy.
However, B does not preserve the Institutes as a continuous, authorially finalized book. Its chapters are scattered through the manuscript, rearranged, and interspersed with other ecclesiastical writings. Modern editors therefore reconstruct a coherent treatise from material that the Worcester compiler transmitted more flexibly. When discussing B, it is safest to describe it not as Wulfstan’s original manuscript but as an important later witness to how his ideas about kingship, episcopal authority, and Christian social order were preserved and reshaped after the Norman Conquest.
